# T1566.003 — Phishing: Spearphishing via Service

| | |
|---|---|
| **MITRE tactic** | Initial Access (ATT&CK); the advisory places the technique under Lateral Movement |
| **Advisory phase** | 10. Lateral Movement |
| **Advisory measure** | 015 — Internal and outbound phishing detection (priority Medium, impact High, effort Medium) |
| **Related techniques** | [T1534](T1534-internal-spearphishing.md) and [T1537](T1537-transfer-data-to-cloud-account.md) — same measure |
| **Status of this detection** | KQL not executed against a production tenant — see [test status](../teststatus.md) |

## Recommendation

> ### `DEFENDER-ALERT + SENTINEL-RULE`
>
> Two Safe Links alerts fire at **High** and are aimed precisely at this
> technique: `A potentially malicious URL click was detected` and `A user clicked
> through to a potentially malicious URL`. Switch them on — they are the best
> there is. But they require **E5/G5 or the Defender for Office 365 Plan 2
> add-on**, they only fire on the click and only for a known malicious URL, and
> they see nothing of the *delivery*: the share invitation, the anonymous link
> or the Teams message that carried the bait. **If you only have E3 without an
> add-on, read this block as `SENTINEL-RULE`** — in that case there is no alert
> and the KQL below is your only coverage.

## Is there a Defender alert for this?

**Partly, and only with E5 or an add-on.**

| Alert policy | Default severity | Licensing | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A potentially malicious URL click was detected` | **High** | E5/G5 or Defender for Office 365 Plan 2 add-on | Fires when a user protected by Safe Links clicks a malicious link. Microsoft: *"This alert is also available in Microsoft Teams scenarios. An alert is generated if a user clicks on a link protected by Safe Links in a Teams chat."* Also looks back 48 hours from the moment the URL verdict changes. Automated investigation: yes. |
| `A user clicked through to a potentially malicious URL` | **High** | E5/G5 or Defender for Office 365 Plan 2 add-on | Fires when the user dismisses the Safe Links warning page and proceeds anyway. Also available for Teams chats. Automated investigation: yes. |

Two caveats that matter in practice:

- **Clicks in Teams channels** do produce an alert, but Microsoft notes:
  *"Clicks on links in Teams channels trigger alerts, but some message metadata
  might not be included."*
- **The delivery remains uncovered.** There is no default alert policy for the
  creation of an anonymous sharing link, for a share invitation to an internal
  colleague, or for a Teams message with a link to a compromised document. Those
  are precisely the three ways in which spearphishing *via service* is
  delivered.

Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-xdr/alert-policies

## Precondition: Safe Links must be enabled for Teams, Office and internal mail

The advisory asks: *"Ensure that security features such as Safe Links and Safe
Attachments are applied consistently to all mail flows within the tenant,
regardless of source or destination."* Translated into the switches in the Safe
Links policy that comes to three, and they are independent of each other:

| Setting in the policy | PowerShell parameter | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Apply Safe Links to email messages sent within the organization** | `EnableForInternalSenders` | *"Select this option to apply the Safe Links policy to messages between internal senders and internal recipients."* |
| **On: Safe Links checks a list of known, malicious links when users click links in Microsoft Teams. URLs are not rewritten.** | `EnableSafeLinksForTeams` | Click protection in Teams. Microsoft: *"This setting might take up to 24 hours to take effect."* |
| **On: Safe Links checks a list of known, malicious links when users click links in Microsoft Office apps. URLs are not rewritten.** | `EnableSafeLinksForOffice` | Click protection in the Office apps — the route along which a shared document delivers its link. |

There is no default Safe Links policy; the preset **Built-in protection** gives
all recipients Safe Links protection, and separate Standard/Strict presets or
custom policies take precedence over it in order. If `EnableSafeLinksForTeams`
or `EnableSafeLinksForOffice` are off, `UrlClickEvents` stays empty for those
channels and the first query below does not work.

Source: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/defender-office-365/safe-links-policies-configure

## Required data source

| Platform | Table | What must be onboarded |
|---|---|---|
| Microsoft Sentinel | `UrlClickEvents`, `EmailEvents`, `EmailUrlInfo` | Data connector **Microsoft Defender XDR** with the Defender for Office 365 tables enabled. |
| Microsoft Sentinel | `OfficeActivity` | Data connector **Microsoft 365** (SharePoint workload) for the sharing events. Requires measure 012 (UAL enabled). |
| Defender XDR advanced hunting | `UrlClickEvents`, `MessageEvents`, `MessageUrlInfo`, `EmailUrlInfo` | No connector. `MessageEvents`/`MessageUrlInfo` contain Teams messages; ZAP for Teams requires Defender for Office 365 Plan 1 or Plan 2. |

## KQL — the core: a click that does not come from email

This is the sharpest query in this file and it requires no tuning at all.
According to Microsoft, the `Workload` column in `UrlClickEvents` takes *"the
values being Email, Office, and Teams"*. Anything that is not `Email` is by
definition a click delivered through a cloud service — the literal definition of
T1566.003.

<!-- query
platform: defender-xdr
name: Malicious or clicked-through link delivered outside email via Teams or an Office app
technique: T1566.003
severity: High
tactics: [InitialAccess, LateralMovement]
interval: PT1H
lookback: P7D
parameters: []
deployable: false
-->

```kql
// Malicious or clicked-through links that were NOT delivered by email.
// Workload = Office or Teams means: the bait arrived through a shared document,
// an Office app or a Teams message.
let lookback = 7d;
UrlClickEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(lookback)
| where Workload in ("Office", "Teams")
| where isnotempty(ThreatTypes) or IsClickedThrough != 0 or ActionType has "Block"
| project Timestamp, AccountUpn, Workload, AppName, AppVersion, ActionType,
          IsClickedThrough, ThreatTypes, DetectionMethods, Url, UrlChain,
          IPAddress, NetworkMessageId, ReportId
| order by Timestamp desc
```

For this table Microsoft notes that clicks from Drafts and Sent Items do not
have a usable `NetworkMessageId`; so do not join on it blindly.

## KQL — Defender XDR: malicious links in Teams

<!-- query
platform: defender-xdr
name: Teams message carrying a URL with a threat verdict
technique: T1566.003
severity: Medium
tactics: [InitialAccess, LateralMovement]
interval: PT1H
lookback: P7D
parameters: []
deployable: false
-->

```kql
// Teams messages with a URL the filter stack has issued a verdict on, plus the
// associated clicks.
let lookback = 7d;
let TeamsUrls =
    MessageUrlInfo
    | where Timestamp > ago(lookback)
    | project Timestamp, TeamsMessageId, Url, UrlDomain, UrlThreat = ThreatTypes;
MessageEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(lookback)
| where isnotempty(ThreatTypes) or DeliveryAction != "Delivered"
| join kind=leftouter TeamsUrls on TeamsMessageId
| project Timestamp, SenderEmailAddress, SenderDisplayName, SenderType,
          IsExternalThread, GroupName, MessageSubject, ThreatTypes,
          DetectionMethods, DeliveryAction, DeliveryLocation, SafetyTip,
          Url, UrlDomain
| order by Timestamp desc
```

`SenderType` and `IsExternalThread` are the BEC-relevant columns here: an
*internal* sender in an *internal* thread with a phish verdict is a compromised
account, not an external attacker.

## KQL — intra-org mail with a link to your own cloud storage

The classic BEC variant: the attacker puts a "secure document" in their own
OneDrive and mails the link around internally. The mail contains no attachment
and no suspicious URL — the domain is, after all, the organisation's own.

<!-- query
platform: defender-xdr
name: Intra-org mail without attachments linking to own SharePoint or OneDrive, sent to many recipients
technique: T1566.003
severity: Low
tactics: [InitialAccess, LateralMovement]
interval: PT1H
lookback: P7D
parameters: [tenantHost]
deployable: false
-->

```kql
// Internal mail without an attachment, with exactly one link to the
// organisation's own SharePoint/OneDrive tenant, to several recipients at once.
let lookback = 7d;
let tenantHost = "yourdomain";       // <-- adjust: the part before .sharepoint.com
let minRecipients = 5;
let Links =
    EmailUrlInfo
    | where Timestamp > ago(lookback)
    | where UrlDomain has_any (strcat(tenantHost, ".sharepoint.com"),
                               strcat(tenantHost, "-my.sharepoint.com"))
    | summarize CloudLinks = count(), Example = any(Url) by NetworkMessageId;
EmailEvents
| where Timestamp > ago(lookback)
| where EmailDirection == "Intra-org"
| where DeliveryAction == "Delivered"
| where AttachmentCount == 0
| join kind=inner Links on NetworkMessageId
| summarize Recipients = dcount(RecipientEmailAddress),
            RecipientList = make_set(RecipientEmailAddress, 30),
            Messages = dcount(NetworkMessageId),
            ExampleLink = any(Example),
            First = min(Timestamp), Last = max(Timestamp)
    by SenderFromAddress, Subject
| where Recipients >= minRecipients
| order by Recipients desc
```

On its own this is noise — that is how collaboration works. It becomes a signal
when it coincides with one of the other techniques in this cluster: the same
sender who shortly beforehand created an anonymous sharing link
([T1537](T1537-transfer-data-to-cloud-account.md)), or who has just put a hiding
rule on their inbox ([T1564.008](T1564.008-email-hiding-rules.md)).

## KQL — Sentinel: anonymous link followed by internal distribution

<!-- query
platform: sentinel
name: Anonymous sharing link created and followed by internal mail to multiple colleagues
technique: T1566.003
severity: Medium
tactics: [InitialAccess, LateralMovement]
interval: PT1H
lookback: P1D
parameters: []
deployable: true
-->

```kql
// Correlation: a user creates an anonymous sharing link and within four hours
// sends internal mail to several colleagues.
let lookback = 1d;
let window = 4h;
let AnonymousLinks =
    OfficeActivity
    | where TimeGenerated > ago(lookback)
    | where Operation =~ "AnonymousLinkCreated"
    | project LinkTime = TimeGenerated, Actor = tolower(UserId),
              File = SourceFileName, Site = Site_Url;
let InternalMail =
    EmailEvents
    | where Timestamp > ago(lookback)
    | where EmailDirection == "Intra-org" and DeliveryAction == "Delivered"
    | summarize Recipients = dcount(RecipientEmailAddress),
                Subject = any(Subject)
        by Sender = tolower(SenderFromAddress), MailTime = bin(Timestamp, 15m)
    | where Recipients >= 3;
AnonymousLinks
| join kind=inner InternalMail on $left.Actor == $right.Sender
| where MailTime between (LinkTime .. (LinkTime + window))
| project LinkTime, MailTime, Actor, File, Site, Subject, Recipients
| order by LinkTime desc
```

## Why this matters for BEC

The advisory describes T1566.003 as *"Use of legitimate cloud services (such as
shared documents) to reach internal targets."* The reason this works is the same
as with [T1534](T1534-internal-spearphishing.md): the message comes from a
trusted colleague, and the bait comes from a trusted domain. There is no
suspicious sender to filter on and no suspicious domain to block — it is your
own tenant.

Two things make this route more attractive than an attachment in BEC:

1. **The mail passes the filter stack clean.** No attachment, no external
   domain, no known malicious URL. What is malicious sits *inside* the document
   or behind a second redirect, and that is only evaluated at the moment of
   clicking — and only if Safe Links for Office apps and Teams is enabled.
2. **The delivery is not a mail event.** A share invitation or a Teams message
   does not end up in `EmailEvents`. Anyone who builds their internal phishing
   detection on mail alone does not see this route at all.

On the advisory's phasing: the advisory places this technique under Lateral
Movement because in BEC it is about reaching the *next* colleague from an
already compromised account. ATT&CK places T1566.003 under Initial Access,
because there the technique describes the first access. Both are correct; the
detection is the same.
